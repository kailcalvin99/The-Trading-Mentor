# The Trading Mentor Constitution

## Why The Trading Mentor Exists

The Trading Mentor exists to help developing traders build discipline, structure, self-awareness, risk control, and market understanding.

It is not a get-rich-quick product.

It is not a signal service.

It is not a casino.

It is a premium trading education and performance-review system.

## Core Promise

The product should help users:

- understand the trading process
- plan before acting
- respect risk
- journal honestly
- review patterns
- learn ICT and SMC concepts responsibly
- reduce impulsive decisions
- become more disciplined over time

The product should never imply that discipline, education, AI, or mentorship guarantees profit.

## User Trust Principle

Trust is the business.

Every feature, page, pricing claim, AI response, and onboarding flow must protect user trust.

The app should make a trader feel more prepared, not more hyped.

## Financial-Safety Principle

The Trading Mentor must stay education-first.

The product may explain concepts, ask review questions, help organize trade plans, calculate position size, highlight risk, and support journaling.

The product must not provide personalized investment advice, guaranteed outcomes, trade signals, or pressure to enter trades.

If a feature touches live trading behavior, the default should be caution, education, and risk review.

## Discipline Principle

The highest-value outcome is not more trades.

The highest-value outcome is better behavior.

The product should reward:

- preparation
- patience
- risk limits
- journaling
- post-trade review
- rule-following
- taking no trade when conditions are unclear

The product should not reward:

- overtrading
- revenge trading
- random entries
- oversized risk
- streak chasing
- daily profit obsession

## Product Principles

- Depth before breadth.
- Reliability before new features.
- Trust before conversion.
- Education before automation.
- Risk controls before excitement.
- Retention before paid ads.
- Simple workflows before clever dashboards.

## Design Principles

The interface should feel:

- premium
- dark
- gold
- focused
- professional
- cinematic
- calm under pressure

The app should feel like a serious trading desk, not an arcade.

Use gold accents with restraint. Use red and green only when they are semantically necessary for risk, loss, profit, or market direction.

Avoid visual noise, cheap glow effects, loud gradients, childish icons, and anything that makes trading feel like entertainment.

## Business Principle

The business should win by creating real user value:

- clear onboarding
- useful free tier
- paid upgrades that unlock serious tools
- transparent pricing
- honest claims
- strong education
- support that builds confidence
- founder-led trust

No dark patterns. No fake scarcity. No misleading income claims.

## Development Principle

The project should move from Replit-built prototype to durable business infrastructure.

Codex should operate through clear lanes, scoped files, automated checks, and founder review.

Documentation and migration planning come before risky deployment changes.

## Success

A successful user interaction leaves the trader feeling:

- calmer
- more disciplined
- clearer on risk
- more honest about behavior
- more capable of reviewing their own process
- less likely to make an impulsive trade

A successful business milestone makes the product:

- more trustworthy
- more stable
- easier to deploy
- easier to support
- easier to explain
- closer to sustainable revenue
